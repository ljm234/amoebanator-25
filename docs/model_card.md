# Model card, Amoebanator MLP triage classifier

Per Mitchell et al., *Model Cards for Model Reporting*, FAT\* 2019
(DOI 10.1145/3287560.3287596). Every fitted value below for the V1.1 model
comes from the files under `outputs/model/` and `outputs/metrics/` that
`scripts/regenerate_all_artifacts.py` writes; the one earlier fitted value
quoted, the pre-V1.1 temperature in Section 9, comes from the repository
history (`docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md` Section 4).

> **Most important limitation.** Run through the full pipeline, 18 of the 30
> bundled rows abstain, and so do 8 of the 11 High rows: 6 at the
> logit-energy gate and 2 at the Mahalanobis gate. Only 3 High rows get a
> High label; none gets a Low label
> (`outputs/metrics/bundled_outcomes.json`). For a must-not-miss triage tool, a
> system that declines on most of the cases it exists to catch has not
> shown it can do its job, and these are rows the model and gates were
> built from (24 of the 30 are its training rows). Section 9 gives the
> detail.

---

## 1. Model details

* **Developer.** Luis Jordan Montenegro-Calla, independent researcher.
  Single-author research codebase.
* **Model date.** Retrained for V1.1 on 2026-09-26 by
  `scripts/regenerate_all_artifacts.py`, after the continuous inputs were
  standardized and training was lengthened to 500 steps. The `model.pt`
  tagged `v1.0.0` and `v1.0.1` was trained on unscaled inputs for 60 steps
  and did not reproduce in the current environment; it remains retrievable
  from the repository history (`docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md` Section 4).
* **Model version.** V1.1 (its release tags are listed on the Releases
  page, https://github.com/ljm234/amoebanator-25/releases). The state_dict at
  `outputs/model/model.pt` and the input scaler at
  `outputs/model/scaler.json` are regenerated by
  `python -m ml.training_calib_dca`, bit for bit in the environment
  recorded in `docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md` Section 2 (on another device the
  weights can differ; Section 3 there).
* **Model type.** Two-class multilayer perceptron (PyTorch); architecture
  `Linear(10, 32) -> ReLU -> Linear(32, 16) -> ReLU -> Linear(16, 2)`, 914
  parameters (`ml/model.py`). Trained with cross-entropy + class weighting;
  a temperature is then fit by L-BFGS on the validation rows (temperature
  scaling, Guo et al. 2017), and the fit leaves it at about 1.0 (Section 9).
* **Training algorithms / parameters.** Adam (lr=1e-3), 500 full-batch
  steps (`TRAIN_EPOCHS` in `ml/training_calib_dca.py`) on the 24-row
  training split of the bundled 30-row dataset. The four continuous inputs
  (`age`, `csf_glucose`, `csf_protein`, `csf_wbc`) are z-scored with the 24
  training rows' means and population standard deviations (`ml/scaling.py`);
  the six binary inputs stay 0/1, and inference applies the saved values
  unchanged. 500 steps is where the training loss has converged (on the
  standardized inputs, about 0.44 after 60 steps and below 0.001 after 500);
  the number was not chosen from any validation or preset result. Class
  weight clamped to `[1, 10]` so tiny datasets do not produce explosive
  losses. Random seed `42`.
* **Features.** Ten tabular features after one-hot expansion of symptoms:
  `age`, `csf_glucose`, `csf_protein`, `csf_wbc`, `pcr`, `microscopy`,
  `exposure`, `sym_fever`, `sym_headache`, `sym_nuchal_rigidity`. Schema
  pinned in `outputs/model/features.json`.
* **Citation / paper / resource.** This repository
  (github.com/ljm234/amoebanator-25); the methods are documented in this
  card and in `docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`. `docs/rare_class_design.md` is the
  protocol for a MIMIC-IV bacterial-vs-viral proxy study, pre-specified but
  not scheduled.
* **License.** Released under the MIT License (see `LICENSE`). The model and
  code are provided for research and education and are not intended or
  validated for clinical or commercial use. See the "License and disclaimer"
  section of `README.md`.
* **Where to send questions.** Single-author project; contact the developer
  through the repository.

## 2. Intended use

* **Primary intended use.** Methodology research on calibration and
  abstention in triage models for low-prevalence neurological
  infections. The classifier exists to exercise the surrounding safety
  stack (temperature scaling, split-conformal abstain, Mahalanobis + energy
  OOD gates, decision curve analysis) on a clinically relevant target. The
  model output is a temperature-scaled probability of the "high-risk" tier
  given a sparse set of presenting features; with the fitted `T` at about
  1.0 it is the model's own softmax output (Section 9). Amoebanator is the
  methodological prototype for the abstention architecture carried into a
  national multicenter network in Peru on opportunistic CNS infection in
  people living with HIV, where a new abstention-aware model is to be
  trained from scratch on the network's own data; that work is separate
  from this repository.
* **Primary intended users.** Methods researchers evaluating the
  calibration / OOD / DCA pipeline. Educators teaching
  rare-disease ML. The Streamlit Predict page exists to make the
  end-to-end pipeline inspectable, not to support any clinical decision.
* **Out-of-scope use cases.**
  - Any real clinical triage decision for an individual patient.
  - Any inferred PAM diagnosis or rule-out.
  - Any deployment in a production EHR or clinical decision-support system.
  - Any substitute for institutional infectious-disease or critical-care
    consultation.

## 3. Factors

* **Relevant factors.** Patient age (the published US PAM cohort in Yoder
  et al. 2010 has a median age of 12 years, range 8 months to 66 years; the
  bundled 30 rows have a median of 23.5 years, range 6 to 62). Sex (the
  published cohort is 79.3 % male; the bundled rows are 16 male and 14
  female). Geographic exposure source (in the published cohort, lakes, ponds
  and reservoirs account for 67 of the 91 cases with a known source,
  73.6 %; the bundled rows have no exposure-source field, only a yes/no
  freshwater `exposure` flag, and the source that most rows with
  `exposure = 1` name in their free-text `comments`, such as a lake, river,
  pond, splash pad, hot spring or nasal rinse, is not a model input). The
  classifier sees age, the CSF values, the PCR, microscopy and exposure
  flags and a symptom checklist; sex is not a model input, and no
  race/ethnicity, socio-economic, or geographic metadata are encoded in the
  bundled features.
* **Evaluation factors.** The current evaluation is *unstratified* by
  demographics because the bundled n = 6 validation split cannot support
  meaningful subgroup analysis. Age-band and sex-stratified evaluation would
  need a real test set for this model, which does not exist. The MIMIC-IV
  bacterial-vs-viral protocol in `docs/rare_class_design.md`, pre-specified
  but not scheduled, would not supply one: it would train and evaluate a
  separate classifier, it specifies no subgroup analysis, and its cohort
  schema has no sex field (`docs/data_card.md`, last section).

## 4. Metrics

* **Performance measures and rationale.** No performance metrics are
  reported, because on six validation rows they would not be meaningful; the
  measures below are the ones a real-data evaluation would report, and no
  such evaluation is scheduled (Section 5).
  - **AUC of the temperature-scaled probability of the High class.**
    Standard discrimination metric for ranked-list triage applications.
  - **Recall (sensitivity) at the operating threshold.** Captures
    miss-rate, the clinically expensive failure mode.
  - **Conformal coverage and abstain rate.** See Section 9, Conformal
    coverage.
  - **Decision-curve net benefit at the chosen threshold.** Compared
    against the treat-all and treat-none baselines.
* **Fitted thresholds.** Out-of-distribution detection is dual-gated:
  a diagonal-covariance Mahalanobis distance on robust (median/MAD)
  z-scores and an energy score on the logits. The Mahalanobis threshold,
  `tau = 21.46`, is the 0.999 quantile of the 24 training rows' own
  distances (`outputs/metrics/feature_stats.json`). The gate is fit on, and
  scores, the raw values of the seven non-symptom inputs (`age`,
  `csf_glucose`, `csf_protein`, `csf_wbc`, `pcr`, `microscopy`,
  `exposure`), before standardization; the three symptom indicators are not
  in this gate. The Predict page sends all seven values with every
  submission, so every submission is scored on all seven terms. The energy
  threshold, `tau = -5.58` (the 0.95 quantile of the validation energies),
  and the conformal threshold, `qhat = 6.80e-05` at `alpha = 1/7`, are
  fitted on the six validation rows
  (`outputs/metrics/energy_threshold.json`, `outputs/metrics/conformal.json`);
  the energy threshold is fitted on the same temperature-scaled logits that
  inference scores.
* **Decision rule.** No fixed operating threshold, such as the 0.5 at which
  `metrics.json` computes recall, decides the label; the conformal step does.
  It predicts High when `p_high >= 1 - qhat` and Low when `p_high <= qhat`,
  and abstains otherwise; with the shipped `qhat` that is High from about
  0.99993 (1 - qhat) and Low up to about 0.000068 (qhat).
* **Uncertainty estimation.** Bootstrap percentile 95 % CI with
  `n_resamples = 2000` available via `ml.metrics.bootstrap.bootstrap_ci`.
  The conformal step abstains when the prediction set is empty or contains
  both classes.

## 5. Evaluation data

* **Datasets used.** A single 6-row stratified validation split from the
  bundled `outputs/diagnosis_log_pro.csv` (30 simulated rows, 80/20
  train/val split at `random_state = 42`); `metrics.json` and `ci.json` are
  computed on it. Section 4 lists which rows fit each threshold; the six
  validation rows are the n = 6 caveat documented in Section 9 and called
  out by `SmallCalibrationWarning` at every conformal fit. Two analyses use
  other rows. The synthetic OOD benchmark (Section 9) scores the shipped
  model's gates on all 30 bundled rows, including the 24 training rows the
  model and the Mahalanobis gate were fit on, against 30 covariate-shifted
  and 30 label-shifted copies of those rows. The ablation (Section 7)
  refits every model on its own 18 / 6 / 6 split of the 30 rows. The
  coverage sweep (Section 7) splits the six validation rows 3 / 3.
* **Motivation.** The goal of V1.0 and V1.1 is an end-to-end
  *infrastructure* (calibration, conformal, OOD, DCA), not a clinically
  valid model. No real evaluation set for this model exists. The MIMIC-IV
  bacterial-vs-viral meningitis protocol in `docs/rare_class_design.md` is
  a pre-specified protocol that is not scheduled; it would test the same
  calibration, abstention and OOD machinery on a separate classifier
  trained on MIMIC-IV, not evaluate this model. PhysioNet credentialed
  access (MIMIC-IV) is in place.
* **Preprocessing.** `pd.read_csv` -> one-hot expansion of `symptoms` ->
  `df.fillna(0)` (`ml.training_calib_dca.load_tabular`) -> 80/20 split ->
  z-scoring of the four continuous inputs with the training rows' means and
  standard deviations (`ml/scaling.py`). The partial Safe Harbor-style
  scrub in `ml/data_loader.load_tabular_safe_harbor` (caps ages at 89,
  blanks `physician`, generalizes dates to year, runs free text > 20 chars
  through a regex scrubber) leaves columns that hold direct identifiers,
  such as `name`, `mrn` or `ssn`, unchanged, so it does not de-identify a
  real-data CSV on its own (`docs/data_card.md` Section 4); the trainer does
  not call it.

## 6. Training data

* **Source.** `outputs/diagnosis_log_pro.csv`, 30 synthetic rows created for
  this demo. Every row carries `source = "simulated"` and
  `physician = "demo"`. No real patient data is included.
* **Distribution over factors.** The age and sex distribution of the 30
  rows does not match the published case series: their median age is 23.5
  years (Yoder JS et al. 2010: 12) and 16 of 30 are male (Yoder: 79.3 %).
  Per-row provenance is carried in the CSV's own `case_id`, `source`,
  `physician` and `timestamp_tz` columns; the audit log
  (`AMOEBANATOR_AUDIT_PATH`) records dataset-level training events (file,
  row and feature counts, split sizes, temperature, model save, validation
  metrics), not per-row provenance.
* **Other relevant details.** Class weight clamped to `[1, 10]` because
  the small training fold has unstable positive counts. The n = 24 train
  fold is fixed by `random_state = 42` in the train/val split, and random
  seed `42`, pinned in `ml/seeds.py`, fixes the weight initialization, so
  training on the same hardware, device and library build gives a
  bit-identical `model.pt`. The input scaler is fit on the 24 training rows
  only.

## 7. Quantitative analyses

* **Unitary results (overall).** Not reported (Section 4).
  `outputs/metrics/metrics.json` and `outputs/metrics/ci.json` hold the AUC
  and recall computed on the n = 6 validation rows; they are not
  performance estimates. The bootstrap behind `ci.json`
  (`scripts/calibration/bootstrap_metrics.py`, 2000 resamples) skips a
  resample in which a metric is undefined and records how many it skipped:
  181 for AUC (one class only) and 174 for recall (no High row). The fitted
  temperature is `T = 0.999997`
  (`outputs/model/temperature_scale.json`), also from those six rows, on
  which it is not identifiable (Section 9).
* **Intersectional results.** Not reported. The validation split has
  insufficient counts to support stratified analysis, and subgroup
  reporting would need a real test set (Section 3).
* **Ablation table.** `outputs/metrics/ablation_table.{json,csv}` holds the
  four-cell ablation (base / +calibration / +conformal / +OOD) for the
  Amoebanator MLP and three calibrated scikit-learn baselines (logistic
  regression + Platt, random forest + isotonic, gradient boosting +
  isotonic), all fit on the ablation's own 18 / 6 / 6 train / calibration /
  test split, the MLP with its own input scaler. Every metric in it is
  computed on at most n = 6 test rows and is not meaningful; the table
  shows that the ablation runs, not how the models compare.
* **Coverage sweep.** `outputs/metrics/coverage_sweep.{json,png}` reports
  empirical coverage at alpha in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20} on a half-half split of
  the validation set (n = 3 calibration / n = 3 test). With three
  calibration points a finite threshold cannot target more than 3/4
  coverage (alpha must be at least 1/4), so the threshold is infinite at
  every alpha the sweep uses, and all three test rows abstain.

## 8. Ethical considerations

* **Sensitive data.** None in the shipped dataset (synthetic, no
  identifiers). The MIMIC-IV protocol in `docs/rare_class_design.md`, which
  is pre-specified but not scheduled, would use MIMIC-IV's de-identified
  records under PhysioNet credentialed access. `ml/irb_gate.py` does not
  treat MIMIC-IV rows as synthetic: its check asks for an IRB record for
  them, as for any real dataset (research mode, which the Docker image sets,
  skips the check and logs the skip). No training entry point calls that
  check; it runs only in `tests/test_irb_gate.py`. No IRB record exists in
  this repository.
* **Model effects on human life / rights / safety.** *Potential* effects in
  the deployment scenario the model targets are catastrophic; PAM is
  near-uniformly fatal. The model card's *Out-of-scope use cases*
  (Section 2) exists to prevent this from being read as a deployable triage
  signal. Misuse risk is concentrated on the demo dashboard: a clinician
  seeing a High result with `p_high` shown as 0.999999 could over-anchor
  without reading the surrounding caveats.
* **Mitigation strategies.** The Mahalanobis gate returns ABSTAIN/OOD when
  an input's distance over the seven non-symptom inputs is above its
  threshold (Section 4), and the energy gate returns
  ABSTAIN/LogitEnergyAboveOODShift when the energy of the temperature-scaled
  logits is above the 95th percentile of the validation energies. An input
  that passes both gates reaches the conformal step, which returns
  ABSTAIN/ConformalEmptySet when its prediction set is empty and
  ABSTAIN/ConformalAmbiguity when the set contains both classes. Every
  ABSTAIN carries a reason field. The Streamlit widget displays the
  research-only disclaimer above the form and the raw safety-signal
  breakdown below the prediction. How well the two gates detect shifted
  inputs is not established: on the bundled synthetic benchmark neither
  separates covariate-shifted rows from the bundled rows (Section 9).
* **Risks and harms (residual).** A reviewer or trainee could read a number
  computed on the six validation rows as a performance estimate, which is
  why Section 4 reports none. The `SmallCalibrationWarning`
  fires at every conformal fit until n >= 100.

## 9. Caveats and recommendations

* **n = 6 validation set.** The main limitation (Section 4). The
  temperature, the conformal qhat and the energy threshold are fitted on
  those six rows; no real held-out set exists for this model (the MIMIC-IV
  protocol in `docs/rare_class_design.md`, pre-specified but not scheduled,
  would calibrate and evaluate a separate classifier, not this one).
* **Temperature.** The raw logits already classify all six validation rows
  correctly, so the validation loss has no finite minimum in `T` and the
  temperature is not identifiable. L-BFGS starts at `T = 1.0` and the fit
  leaves it at `T = 0.999997` (`outputs/model/temperature_scale.json`),
  essentially that starting value, because the loss is already near zero
  there; the clamp `[0.1, 10]` is not reached. The probabilities the app
  shows are therefore the model's own softmax outputs, and nothing in this
  release shows them to be well calibrated. Before V1.1, with unscaled
  inputs and 60 training steps, the fitted `T` was about 0.27, which
  sharpened the probabilities toward 0 and 1.
* **Synthetic training data.** All 30 rows carry `source = "simulated"`. The
  model has never seen a real patient. A separate synthesis function
  (`ml/case_series.synthesize_yoder_cohort`, used only by the tests, not to
  build the 30 rows) draws sex and exposure source with the Yoder 2010
  frequencies and age from a log-normal around the Yoder 2010 median of 12
  years, clipped to the published range (the spread is not published);
  rows it produces carry `source = "synthetic_from_yoder2010"` so they can
  be filtered out of any real-data quoted metric.
* **Conformal coverage.** Calibration, the conformal threshold and the
  energy gate share six validation rows; the Mahalanobis gate is fit on the
  24 training rows. Split conformal coverage still holds on average under
  exchangeability, but with six points the realized coverage varies widely,
  and a valid threshold cannot target more than 6/7 coverage, about 86
  percent, without abstaining on every input. The demo therefore targets
  alpha = 1/7 (`config/amoebanator.toml`); at alpha = 0.10 the threshold
  would be infinite and every input would abstain.
* **No bacterial / viral / fungal class labels.** The 30 rows contain no
  bacterial meningitis that is not PAM, so the model cannot tell bacterial
  meningitis from PAM. The app's bacterial-meningitis preset gets
  `p_high = 0.9994` and abstains at the logit-energy gate
  (`LogitEnergyAboveOODShift`); that gate also flags 15 of the 30 bundled
  rows (`outputs/metrics/bundled_outcomes.json`), so the abstention is not
  recognition of bacterial meningitis.
  Across the full pipeline, 18 of the 30 bundled rows abstain (15 at the
  logit-energy gate, 3 at the Mahalanobis gate), including 8 of the 11 High
  rows, and only 3 High rows get a High label
  (`outputs/metrics/bundled_outcomes.json`; see the limitation stated at
  the top of this card). The
  MIMIC-IV protocol (`docs/rare_class_design.md` Section 4) pre-specifies a
  real OOD evaluation (bacterial and viral meningitis test rows vs B60.2 PAM
  rows) of a separate bacterial-vs-viral classifier trained on MIMIC-IV, not
  of this model; it is not scheduled. PhysioNet credentialing is in place.
  The bundled synthetic OOD benchmark
  (`outputs/metrics/synthetic_ood_benchmark.json`, the 30 bundled rows
  against 30 shifted copies) reports AUC 0.50 on label shift, as expected,
  because flipping labels does not change the feature distribution. On its
  covariate shift (CSF glucose, protein and WBC multiplied by random factors
  in [0.5, 2.0], plus noise) the AUC is 0.561 for the Mahalanobis distance,
  0.568 for the logit energy and 0.445 for the secondary negative-energy
  signal, which does not abstain. At the fitted
  thresholds the Mahalanobis gate flags 13 % of the shifted rows and 10 % of
  the bundled rows, and the logit-energy gate 53 % and 50 %: neither gate
  separates that shift from the bundled rows.
* **Cope 2016 CSF numerics not encoded.** The Cope JR & Ali IK 2016 review
  reports CSF abnormalities qualitatively only. For numeric PAM CSF values,
  Capewell LG et al. 2015 (PMID 26582886), which reports medians and ranges
  of CSF WBC, glucose and protein for U.S. PAM cases, is the better source;
  integrating it into `ml/case_series.py` is documented as optional future
  work.
* **No neonatal PAM in the corpus.** The 60-vignette PAM corpus
  (`data/vignettes/pam/`, which this model is neither trained nor
  evaluated on) does not include extreme-age (neonatal) PAM presentations,
  because neonatal PAM is a distinct epidemiologic category that does not
  fit the cluster structure (lake_pond, river, splash_pad, nasal_irrigation,
  hot_springs, pakistan_ablution, latam, survivor_adult,
  survivor_pediatric). Reserved for a future extension. **Model
  performance on real PAM, neonatal or not, is undefined; the model has not
  been evaluated on a real PAM case.** Each vignette's cluster is set in
  `scripts/vignettes/generate_pam_vignettes.py` and recorded in its
  `provenance` field.
* **Recommendations.**
  - Do not quote AUC, recall, conformal coverage, or DCA net benefit
    computed on the six validation rows.
  - Do not deploy the Streamlit widget on any clinical-facing surface.
  - After a re-fit, regenerate every artifact with
    `scripts/regenerate_all_artifacts.py` and update every value this card
    quotes from the regenerated files. The outcome of each bundled row is
    in `outputs/metrics/bundled_outcomes.json`, and so are the counts
    derived from it: the abstention counts quoted at the top of this card,
    in Section 9, in `docs/tripod-ai.md` and in the README's Limitations,
    and the number of bundled rows the logit-energy gate flags (15 of the
    30), quoted in Section 9, `docs/tripod-ai.md`,
    `docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md` and the bacterial preset's banner
    (`app/presets.py`). `tests/test_bundled_outcomes.py` fails when any of
    these quotes no longer matches the artifact. Recompute the ones no artifact
    stores: run `ml.infer.infer_one` on the regenerated artifacts for the
    bacterial preset's `p_high` and the gate at which it abstains
    (Section 9), and measure the training losses quoted in Section 1 again,
    because the pipeline does not write them.
